# Analysis: "Top-layer structures across 12 finance-first and adjacent business platforms"

Assessment of `finance-platform-top-layer-research-paper.md` against Hleb's stated constraints and `docs/research/finance-first-architecture.md` (sections 1, 2, 6.1, 6.8). Verification done by independent WebSearch/WebFetch this session; the paper's own citation marks (`[web:n]`, `[page:n]`, `[file:84]`) do not resolve to a bibliography and were not used as evidence.

## 1. Executive summary

The paper surveys 12 systems (Agicap, SAP S/4HANA, Dynamics 365 Finance, Odoo, Frappe/ERPNext, Anaplan, Ramp, Brex, NetSuite, Rillet, ERP5, Tryton) and concludes there is no universal top layer: each vendor organizes around a different "money truth" (statutory, treasury, spend, planning, project). It explicitly declines to propose a final taxonomy, offering seven candidate top-layer domains instead (Accounting, Treasury, FP&A, Spend, CRM/RevOps, Projects, Operations).

It is usable as a raw evidence inventory once its citation marks are replaced with real URLs and its labels corrected (several "documented"-grade claims are actually vendor marketing pages). It is not usable as a taxonomy decision: its final candidate list closely mirrors Hleb's own iteratively-revised list from the Perplexity transcript, its source set (12) is far short of the 20+ he asked for, named systems from his own transcript (Oracle, Unit4, Acumatica, SAP Business One) are missing, and no Czech/EU SME tool appears despite that being Afframe's market. This repeats the anchoring pattern he flagged twice in that transcript. Treat it as one input, not as validation.

## 2. The paper's thesis, method, sources, top-layer structure

**Thesis:** top-layer product boundaries follow distinct "money truths" (statutory/accounting, treasury/cash, spend control, planning/FP&A, project economics), not a universal ERP checklist; shared reference data (partners, locations, chart of accounts, cost centers) is a separate axis from sellable products and should be centralized.

**Method:** for each of 12 systems, answer five questions (what top layer is exposed, which parts are money-related, apparent core domain, why structured this way, what lesson it implies), reproduced from "the research brief" (`[file:84]`), plus cross-system synthesis.

**Sources:** the paper cites three kinds of tags with no resolving bibliography anywhere in the document: `[file:84]` (the originating brief, 79 occurrences), `[web:n]` (157 occurrences across 25 distinct ids), `[page:n]` (41 occurrences across 3 distinct ids: 7, 9, 10), and one `[memory:1]`. None of these tags link to an actual URL in this file.

**Top-layer structure it proposes** (Synthesis section): Accounting (statutory records, close, reconciliation, reporting); Treasury/Cash Management (liquidity, forecast cash); FP&A/Planning (versions, scenarios, assumptions); Spend or Procurement Finance (authorization, policy, spend routing, settlement handoff); CRM/Revenue Operations (commercial pipeline); Projects/Project Economics (delivery-linked cost/revenue); Operations or Supply-related execution (commitments, stock, fulfilment). Shared directories/master data are proposed as a separate, centralized, non-sellable foundation.

## 6. Quality problems

1. **No resolvable bibliography.** 79 `[file:84]`, 157 `[web:n]` across 25 distinct ids, 41 `[page:n]` across 3 distinct ids, and one `[memory:1]` tag appear in the text with no reference list anywhere in the document mapping them to actual URLs. Every claim in section 3 above had to be re-verified from scratch this session rather than checked against the paper's own sources.
2. **Sections resting entirely on the brief re-citing itself.** The Dynamics 365, Ramp (partly), ERP5, and Tryton sections cite `[file:84]` almost exclusively, meaning the paper is restating the originating research brief's content as though it were independently sourced, not adding new verification.
3. **Signs of unedited conversational output rather than an independent paper.** A stray `[memory:1]` tag (an artifact of a conversational memory system), direct second-person address to the reader ("the risk you have repeatedly flagged," line 80), and an unexplained orphan tag `[web:16]` with no surrounding context all indicate this is lightly-edited assistant output rather than a standalone research document.
4. **Narrow, self-confirming coverage.** 12 systems against the 20+ Hleb asked for; Oracle, Unit4, Acumatica, and SAP Business One (all present in his own earlier transcript) are missing, and no Czech/EU SME tool is covered despite that being Afframe's actual market.
5. **Selective evidence within its own sources.** The paper foregrounds evidence for keeping FP&A, Treasury, Spend, and Accounting separate, but doesn't surface counter-evidence sitting inside its own citations: SAP's Financial Management line of business bundles FI/CO with Treasury and FP&A (per verification claim 2's sources); Odoo files Accounting under one "Finance" app category (claim 3); Anaplan lists "Financial Consolidation" under its Finance planning apps (claim 6). None of these are fatal to the paper's thesis, but their absence makes the paper read as confirming a preferred structure rather than testing it.
6. **States it is not a decision document, but is easy to mistake for one.** The paper explicitly declines to propose a final taxonomy (lines 15, 204), offering candidates only — worth restating plainly since the Synthesis section's phrasing ("the strongest recurring candidates") could be read as a recommendation.
7. **Missing vendors named in this task's own brief.** Oracle and Xero, both named as vendors to check in this task, are not covered anywhere in the paper. Xero in particular is a stronger analog than Rillet for "Accounting can stand alone as a complete flagship product" in Afframe's actual SME segment (Rillet targets mid-market SaaS/subscription companies).

## 7. What to keep, what to discard, open decisions for Hleb

**Keep**
- The five-truths lens (statutory / treasury / spend / planning / project) as a checklist for scoring future candidates — costs nothing, adds no commitment.
- The distinction between sellable top-layer products and centralized shared reference data as a named design axis, separate from ownership of records.
- The nine vendor facts re-verified in section 3 (Odoo, ERPNext, NetSuite, Agicap, Anaplan, Brex, Rillet, Ramp, SAP modules), now attached to real URLs, as raw material for an evidence file.

**Discard**
- The Synthesis section's seven-candidate list as a settled taxonomy — treating it as more than a hypothesis imports the anchoring problem in section 6.4.
- The Dynamics 365, Ramp, ERP5, and Tryton sections as currently written — they add no evidence beyond the brief they cite; either re-source them from primary vendor documentation or drop them.
- The bracket-citation style without a bibliography — any reuse of this paper's claims should carry real URLs, or the next reader repeats this session's verification work from zero.
