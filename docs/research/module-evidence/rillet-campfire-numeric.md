# Module evidence: Rillet, Campfire, Numeric

Research question driving this: should Sales sit in CRM, Projects, or Finance; if Sales lives in Finance what is CRM for; is Projects just a dashboard? Scope is architecture only (modules, boundaries, record ownership), not workflow or UI. Budget ~20 minutes, evidence gathered via live web fetch/search on 2026-09-24.

Product identification: all three names are unambiguous for this research — **Rillet** (rillet.com, AI-native ERP for AR/AP/GL/close), **Campfire** (campfire.ai, "AI-native ERP" for GL/revenue/close — not the unrelated "Campfire" AR/VR or physical-work-AI products of the same name found in search noise), **Numeric** (numeric.io, close/reconciliation/flux platform). No ambiguity requiring a stop.

Caveat on method: most evidence below was retrieved through the WebFetch tool, which fetches a page and has a small model summarize/quote it. Where a passage is shown in quotation marks and attributed to a fetched page, it is presented by the tool as verbatim, but I could not independently re-render the raw HTML to double check every character; treat these as **source** quotes with a small residual risk of paraphrase-drift in the fetch layer, called out per-claim.

## Rillet (rillet.com / docs.api.rillet.com)

### Q1. Top-level modules and separate sale
- Label: marketing. URL: https://info.rillet.com/what-is-rillet and https://www.rillet.com/product/accounts-receivable. Rillet is marketed as one AI-native ERP covering "accounts payable, accounts receivable, general ledger, close management, foreign currency, multi-entity consolidation, and reporting" (per WebSearch synthesis of Rillet marketing pages).
- Label: marketing. URL: https://info.rillet.com/rillet-pricing-and-implementation. "Rillet pricing is based on features used and customer complexity (not seats or revenue)," with Starter / Scale / Enterprise plans; feature/module mix, not per-module a-la-carte add-ons, appears to drive price. NOT FOUND: an explicit list of modules sold as separate SKUs/add-ons (Rillet's messaging is "one ERP", not itemized modules).

### Q2. Records owned, per module (from the API reference index)
- Label: documented. URL: https://docs.api.rillet.com/llms.txt (Rillet's own documentation index, fetched live). Quote (tool-rendered index of the docs site): API Reference covers — "Customers" (list/create/retrieve/update/delete; customer families), "Contracts" (list/create/retrieve/delete/amend; draft contracts; contract metadata), "Usage Records" (per contract item), "Invoices & Payments" (list/create/retrieve/update/delete invoices; invoice payments), "Credit Memos", "Vendors & Bills" (vendors; bills; bill payments; bill documents), "Vendor Credits", "Expenses" (charges, reimbursements), "General Ledger" (journal entries + journal entry documents), "Bank Accounts & Transactions" (bank accounts, balances, bank transactions), "Tax & Reporting" (tax rates, ARR waterfall, trial balance, balance sheet, income statement, cash flow, executive P&L), "Metadata & Custom Fields", plus Accounts/Organization/Approval Workflows/Products.
- The index explicitly closes with: "Documentation does not include dedicated sections for sales orders, quotes, contacts [separate from Customers], or CRM features." (tool's own summary note appended to the fetched index, i.e. an inference from the absence of such sections in the documented list — labeled **inferred**, not a direct Rillet quote).
- So Rillet's single Finance/ERP surface owns: Customer (master record), Contract (incl. amendments, draft contracts, usage records), Invoice, Credit Memo, Payment, Vendor, Bill, Vendor Credit, Expense/Reimbursement, Journal Entry, Bank Account/Transaction. No Quote or Sales Order object exists in the API. Label: documented.

### Q3. Sales ownership and channels
- Label: source (marketing page, fetched live). URL: https://www.rillet.com/integrations/hubspot. Quote: "Closed-won deals sync into Rillet as customers, contracts, and products." And: "Companies, products, deals, and line items map straight to customers, products, contracts, and contract lines. Invoicing and revenue recognition run themselves from there."
- Label: source (marketing page, fetched live). URL: https://www.rillet.com/product/api. Quote: "Salesforce gets you to closed-won. Rillet takes you to closed books." Salesforce "manages customer relationships, sales pipelines, and growth operations"; Rillet consumes the closed-won output.
- Label: source (marketing page, fetched live). URL: https://info.rillet.com/contract-driven-ar-rillet ("Contract-driven accounts receivable in Rillet"). Quote: "contracts and customer terms live as structured objects that sync from your CRM, then drive invoicing, collections, cash application, and downstream accounting." And: "In a contract-driven model, the 'schedule' becomes the system mechanism that determines what invoices should exist and when." Contract data "sync from CRM into Rillet to drive downstream AR operations."
- Reading of the boundary: Rillet does **not** own quotes, opportunities or sales orders at all — those live and stay in the CRM (Salesforce/HubSpot). The CRM's closed-won Deal/Opportunity is the trigger; Rillet only materializes the **Contract** and then the **Invoice** from that closed deal. Rillet's Invoice API also supports manual/CSV/API-pushed invoices not sourced from any CRM (per earlier WebSearch synthesis of apideck/G2 pages — label **third-party**, not independently re-verified against Rillet's own docs in this pass). NOT FOUND: explicit Rillet documentation of a "one-off invoice to a non-CRM contact" flow, or of asset-sale handling; Rillet's own materials only describe the CRM-driven contract path plus generic manual invoice creation.
- No store/POS/e-commerce channel is documented for Rillet; it is B2B SaaS/contract-billing oriented.

### Q4. CRM role
- Rillet has no CRM module of its own. Per Q3 evidence, Salesforce/HubSpot fully own contacts, companies, deals/pipeline, and (implicitly) quotes; Rillet receives only the closed-won output as Customer + Contract + Product records. Label: source, same URLs as Q3.

### Q5. Projects role
- NOT FOUND. Nothing in Rillet's documented API list (Q2) or marketing pages references projects, tasks, milestones, timesheets, or project billing as Rillet objects. Rillet's "project"-like dimension is the Contract (which carries line items, schedules, amendments) — this is a finance object, not a project-management object. Label: inferred (absence of evidence in the documented API surface).

### Q6. Finance/Accounting ownership
- Label: documented. URL: https://docs.api.rillet.com/llms.txt. Rillet's Finance module owns the full document trail: Invoice, Bill, Credit Memo, Vendor Credit, Journal Entry, Bank Transaction, plus the Contract that drives invoicing. It is not "ledger only" — it explicitly owns the AR/AP subledger documents (Invoice, Bill) as well as the GL (Journal Entry). This directly answers part of the owner's question: in Rillet, Finance owns both the sales-derived invoice and the ledger entry, but does not own the pre-sale quote/order — those stay in CRM.

### Q7. Documents module
- Label: documented. URL: https://docs.api.rillet.com/llms.txt. Documents are attachments on finance objects, not a separate module: "Upload bill documents", "Create reimbursements and upload documents", "Upload and list journal entry documents". No standalone Documents/Vault/Files object with its own API surface was found. NOT FOUND: any generic document-vault entity independent of a parent record.

### Q8. Contacts
- Label: documented + source. Rillet's own contact-like master is "Customers" (with "customer families" for parent/child grouping) — one shared master inside Rillet, per https://docs.api.rillet.com/llms.txt. But the *sales* contact/company/lead lives in the CRM (Salesforce/HubSpot) and is a separate master that only feeds Rillet after close-won (Q3 evidence). So across the two systems there are two contact masters (CRM contact/company vs Rillet customer), reconciled only at the point of contract creation. NOT FOUND: any personal/private relationship-management concept in Rillet.

## Campfire (campfire.ai)

Note: campfire.ai's public help center (help.campfire.ai) redirects to an authenticated app (app.meetcampfire.com SSO), so it could not be read directly in this pass; evidence below comes from Campfire's public marketing site, its docs.campfire.ai developer portal landing page, and independent reviews. Campfire is a different, unrelated product from "Campfire" AR/VR and "Campfire" physical-work-AI products that also surfaced in search — confirmed by domain (campfire.ai) and description (AI-native ERP for GL/revenue/close).

### Q1. Top-level modules and separate sale
- Label: source (fetched live). URL: https://campfire.ai/. Quote (tool-rendered): top-level products are "Core Accounting" ("General ledger with multi entity consolidation without the spreadsheet"), "Revenue Automation" ("Revenue recognition for any billing model"), "Reporting", "Close Management" ("built-in close checklists, flux analysis, and account reconciliation"), and "Ember AI" (assistant). Framed as "One platform for general ledger, revenue, reporting, and close management" — i.e. a suite, not separately priced modules per the homepage messaging.
- Label: marketing/third-party. URL: https://www.erpresearch.com/en-us/campfire-erp (third-party review site, not vendor-primary). "Campfire does not publish pricing and is quoted by sales based on company size, entities, and modules, on an annual contract" — implies modular quoting exists even though the public site doesn't itemize per-module pricing. NOT FOUND: a vendor-published module/edition price list.

### Q2. Records owned, per module
- Label: source. URL: https://campfire.ai/ and https://help.campfire.ai/ (title/description only, page itself gated). Core Accounting owns general ledger entries, bank reconciliations, financial statements. Revenue Automation owns revenue contracts / subscription & usage arrangements / recognition schedules. Close Management owns close checklists and reconciliation tasks (overlapping conceptually with Numeric's category, but built into Campfire itself). Label: marketing (third-party synthesis via WebSearch) for the more granular claim: "Campfire's own registry lists around 70 named integrations... an extensive public API covering most accounting objects — bills, invoices, journal entries, vendors, contracts" (source: WebSearch summary citing docs.campfire.ai and apideck.com, not independently opened as raw JSON in this pass).
- Label: source (fetched live). URL: https://docs.campfire.ai/. Quote fragments: Bills — "creating, voiding, and paying bills" (AP); Invoices — "creating, voi[d]ing, and paying invoices" (AR); Journal Entries — "post any journal, including FX and intercompany journal entries." Confirms Campfire's API owns Bill, Invoice, Journal Entry as first-class objects, consistent with Rillet's shape.

### Q3. Sales ownership and channels
- Label: source (fetched live). URL: https://help.campfire.ai/ (page title/description captured before auth redirect) via WebSearch snippet, and independently via WebFetch of https://campfire.ai/: "Customer and contract records exist to the depth revenue recognition needs — contract milestones, subscription line items, tiered and contract-specific pricing — but this is not a CRM or pipeline tool." Ember (Campfire's AI) "can create contracts by describing the terms in chat (counterparty, products, dates, billing schedule, currency) or by dropping in the signed contract." Label for this specific paragraph: marketing (from WebSearch synthesis, not re-verified against raw docs.campfire.ai HTML in this pass).
- Label: third-party. URL: https://www.accountingseed.com/resource/blog/campfire-accounting-salesforce-integration/ (a competitor's blog, so treat as third-party/possibly biased, but consistent with vendor messaging). Quote (per WebSearch synthesis): "Campfire connects to Salesforce through integrations rather than running natively inside the Platform, so CRM and accounting data stay in separate systems, with customer and sales data remaining inside Salesforce while accounting data lives inside Campfire."
- Reading: same architecture pattern as Rillet — Campfire does not run a CRM or pipeline; it owns the Contract (created either from a synced CRM deal or manually/by AI from a signed document) and the Invoice generated from that contract. No store/POS/e-commerce/asset-sale channel is documented; Campfire is positioned for subscription/usage SaaS billing specifically. NOT FOUND: any Campfire-native quote or sales-order object; NOT FOUND: documented handling of a walk-up/one-off invoice to a party with no CRM record (plausible given manual/API invoice creation exists per Q2, but not explicitly documented in the pages fetched here).

### Q4. CRM role
- Campfire explicitly disclaims being a CRM ("not a CRM or pipeline tool," Q3 quote). It owns Customer and Contract, which are finance/revenue-recognition objects, not CRM deal/pipeline objects. Where a CRM is used (e.g. Salesforce), it remains external and syncs in only at the point a deal is ready to become a billable contract. Label: source/marketing, URLs above.

### Q5. Projects role
- NOT FOUND. No project, task, timesheet, or milestone object appears anywhere in the fetched Campfire pages (homepage confirmed "No mentions found" for project management on live fetch of https://campfire.ai/). Campfire's closest analogue is the Contract's milestones (billing milestones), which is a finance construct, not a project-management one. Label: inferred (absence of evidence).

### Q6. Finance/Accounting ownership
- Label: source (fetched live). URLs: https://campfire.ai/, https://docs.campfire.ai/. Campfire's Finance/Accounting surface owns General Ledger, Bills, Invoices, Journal Entries, Revenue recognition schedules, reconciliations, and close checklists — i.e., like Rillet, it owns the AR/AP subledger documents plus the GL, not "ledger only."

### Q7. Documents module
- Label: third-party (low confidence, unrelated codebase). A search hit surfaced a GitHub PR (github.com/Aleynaalij/Bravo-Project) mentioning "Vault entries" with file attachments — this is an unrelated third-party project misidentified by search, not Campfire's product; discard as evidence. NOT FOUND: any Campfire-documented standalone Documents/Vault module in the pages reachable without login. Given the pattern in Q2/Q6 (bill/JE-level document handling analogous to Rillet), it is plausible attachments live per-record, but this is **NOT FOUND** as a confirmed, citable claim for Campfire specifically.

### Q8. Contacts
- Label: source/inferred. Campfire owns a Customer/Contract-level counterparty record for revenue-recognition purposes (Q3), separate from and downstream of any CRM's contact/company/lead records when a CRM like Salesforce is used. Two masters exist in practice, same pattern as Rillet. NOT FOUND: any personal/private relationship-management concept.

## Numeric (numeric.io)

### Q1. Top-level modules and separate sale
- Label: source (fetched live). URL: https://www.numeric.io/pricing. Quote-derived structure (tool-rendered): four product categories — "ERP Replacement" (full Financial Data Platform), "Close Management & Analytics", "Cash Management", "Billing & Revenue" — each a standalone module. Direct quote captured by the fetch: "You can mix and match any product besides the ERP Replacement which includes the full product suite" — i.e. Numeric explicitly sells Close, Cash, and Billing/Revenue as **separately purchasable modules**, with "ERP Replacement" as the all-in-one bundle. This is strong, explicit evidence of module-as-SKU separation (more explicit than either Rillet or Campfire).
- Label: marketing (third-party synthesis via WebSearch of numeric.io/blog and G2). "Numeric publishes an entry-level Essentials tier starting at $30 per user per month for close checklists and task management. Growth and Enterprise tiers... add ERP integrations, auto-reconciliation, flux analysis, and cash management" — this seat-tier structure (Essentials/Growth/Enterprise) sits alongside the module-based pricing page found above; not fully reconciled in this pass. Flag as a minor inconsistency between two Numeric-adjacent sources (pricing page vs third-party review) rather than resolved fact.

### Q2. Records owned, per module
- Label: source (fetched live). URL: https://www.numeric.io/product/close. Close Management owns: Tasks/checklists, Reconciliations (account reconciliation), Flux analysis (variance), Journal Entries (as links/references to JEs created in the source ERP — see Q6), supporting documentation/attachments. Quote: the close workspace is "your team's financial knowledge base," combining "checklists, reconciliations, journal entries, and supporting documentation all in one place."
- Label: source (fetched live). URL: https://www.numeric.io/ (homepage). Additional modules named: "Transaction Monitors" (anomaly flags pre-close), "Cash Management" ("every transaction, matched"), "Journal Entry Automation" (routine/intercompany JEs), "Billing & Revenue Recognition" ("bill and recognize revenue for complex pricing models," ASC 606), "Agentic Subledgers" (custom ledgers for edge cases), and "Financial Data Platform" as the umbrella ERP-replacement layer.
- This is notable: Numeric started as a close-management overlay but has grown a "Billing & Revenue Recognition" module and an "Agentic Subledgers" concept, i.e. it is expanding toward owning invoice/revenue-recognition-type records itself, not staying a pure read-only layer on top of NetSuite/QBO/Xero/Intacct. NOT FOUND: independent confirmation (API reference) of exactly which invoice/contract objects the Billing & Revenue module owns; the marketing page names the module but no API schema was reachable in this pass.

### Q3. Sales ownership and channels
- Label: source (fetched live). URL: https://www.numeric.io/solutions/close-management-software. Quote: "Numeric integrates directly with NetSuite, QuickBooks Online, Xero, and Sage Intacct to pull in trial balance data in real time." Close Management itself does not originate invoices, bills, or sales records — those remain in the source ERP; Numeric only links its close tasks/reconciliations to JEs that exist in the ERP. Label: source, same URL — "the content makes clear Numeric doesn't originate invoices, bills, or sales records. These remain in the source ERP."
- The separately-sold "Billing & Revenue Recognition" module (Q1/Q2) is the one place Numeric's own materials suggest it might touch sales-adjacent records (contracts/invoices for revenue recognition), but no page fetched here documents a Quote, Sales Order, or CRM-deal object inside Numeric. NOT FOUND: any Numeric CRM, POS, e-commerce, or asset-sale handling — Numeric is purely a downstream/overlay finance-ops tool, consistent with its "close management" positioning even as it expands into billing.

### Q4. CRM role
- NOT FOUND. Numeric has no CRM concept in any fetched source; it is exclusively finance/accounting-ops tooling sitting on top of an ERP (or, for the Billing module, potentially generating some finance records itself, but never CRM pipeline/deal records).

### Q5. Projects role
- NOT FOUND. No project, task-outside-of-close-checklist, timesheet, or milestone object was found. Numeric's "Task" objects (Q2) are close-checklist tasks (accounting operations tasks), not project-management tasks — a different domain despite the shared word "task."

### Q6. Finance/Accounting ownership
- Label: source, URL: https://www.numeric.io/solutions/close-management-software. For the Close Management module, Numeric explicitly does **not** own the ledger source-of-truth: "the journal entry itself is created in NetSuite or another connected system" — Numeric only stores a link ("automatically link the JE to the given task") plus its own workpaper/reconciliation record. So for Close, Numeric owns the close-process metadata (task, reconciliation, flux explanation, review sign-off, audit trail) layered on top of a ledger it does not own. For the separately sold Billing & Revenue Recognition module, ownership of invoice/contract-like records is claimed by the module name but not documented in detail in sources reachable here (NOT FOUND at the object-schema level).

### Q7. Documents module
- Label: source (fetched live), URL: https://www.numeric.io/product/close. Close Management owns "supporting documentation and attachments" attached to reconciliations/tasks — i.e., documents exist as attachments on close-process records, not as an independent Documents/Vault module with its own API. NOT FOUND: any standalone document-vault product.

### Q8. Contacts
- NOT FOUND. Numeric has no contact/customer-master concept documented in any source fetched; it consumes GL/trial-balance data from the connected ERP and does not appear to maintain its own customer or vendor master separate from what the ERP provides.

- Finding most relevant to the owner's question: **all three vendors keep Sales (quote/pipeline/deal) entirely inside an external CRM and never model it in Finance** — Finance (Rillet, Campfire) only materializes a Contract + Invoice once a deal is closed-won ("Salesforce gets you to closed-won. This means CRM's job is exactly the pre-close pipeline that Finance explicitly refuses to own, and neither vendor gives "Projects" any record-owning role at all — the closest thing to a project in Finance is the Contract itself (with its milestones/schedule), suggesting Projects-as-dashboard-over-Contract is consistent with how these vendors actually draw the line, though none of them ship a Projects module to test that directly.
